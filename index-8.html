The science

Iron, folic acid & vitamin C work as a team

Eating iron is only half the story. How much reaches your blood depends on what you eat with it. Tap a card to learn more.

🩸

Iron

Builds haemoglobin
Iron forms haemoglobin, which carries oxygen in red blood cells. Plant foods contain non-heme iron, which is absorbed less easily than the heme iron in animal foods — so pairing matters.
🍋

Vitamin C

Unlocks plant iron
Vitamin C converts non-heme iron into a form the gut absorbs more easily. Squeeze lemon on iron-rich food, or add tomato, capsicum, amla or coriander.
🥬

Folic acid (folate)

Makes healthy red cells
Folate is needed for DNA synthesis and for red blood cells to mature. Low folate causes megaloblastic anaemia. Find it in green leafy vegetables, pulses and sprouts.
☕

Caffeine restriction

Tea & coffee block iron
Polyphenols and tannins in tea and coffee bind iron and cut its absorption. Avoid them with meals and keep a gap of about 1–2 hours.

Who needs it most

Pregnant mothers & adolescent girls

Anaemia is common in women and girls across India, including Bengal. Needs for iron and folate rise sharply at these life stages.

Pregnancy raises the demand

  • Blood volume expands, and the baby needs iron too
  • Folic acid helps prevent neural tube defects
  • Vitamin C improves absorption of plant iron
  • Take iron-folic acid (IFA) tablets as advised by the doctor

Growth years, higher needs

  • Rapid growth plus monthly blood loss after menarche
  • Good iron stores now protect future pregnancies
  • Weekly IFA supplements are offered through school and government programmes
  • Iron-rich snacks in tiffin help fill the gap

Alternative diet

Options for every plate

🌱

Vegetarian

Plant-based iron
Combine pulses, leafy greens, seeds and jaggery with a vitamin-C source at every meal.
🐟

Non-vegetarian

Heme iron
Liver, eggs, small fish and meat supply heme iron, which the body absorbs best.
🍳

Cooking tips

Raise availability
Cooking in an iron kadai, sprouting pulses and fermenting batters can improve iron availability.
🚫

Avoid with meals

Absorption blockers
Tea, coffee and calcium supplements taken with meals can reduce iron absorption.
